How the model works and what to trust

Sunlight: UV index = 12.5 · cos(zenith)^2.42 · (ozone/300)^-1.23 (Allaart et al. 2004 approximation), times Earth-Sun distance, +10% per km altitude, ground reflection, a cloud factor 1 − opacity · cover^2.5 (an empirical shape), and the barrier. Dose = UV index × 1.5 J/m² per minute (erythemal).

Damage: each cell gets Poisson-random thymine dimers. Nucleotide excision repair removes each one with a probability that saturates as damage rises and grows with dark repair time. Photolyase adds light-driven repair. Unrepaired dimers are counted per cell.

Death: a cell dies if unrepaired dimers exceed its tolerance. Tolerance is higher in S/G2 cells (sister chromatid recombination), diploids, and stationary-phase cells. Because it is a threshold on a random count, the survival curve shows a shoulder, then a steep drop.

Natural variation: pipetting (Poisson cells per plate), 5% non-viable cells, per-cell repair capacity, clumps shielding each other, and passing clouds changing the dose.

Limits: constants (damage yield, repair rates, tolerance, cloud shape) are calibrated approximations, not measurements. Use it to explore trends and compare with real data, not to predict exact numbers. Yeast are single cells, so skin layers, melanin, and immune response are not modeled.
